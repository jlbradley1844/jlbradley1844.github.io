## What Next

Well, the past week has been, ummmm educational. I'm not going to say how, explicitly.
As a Baha'i, there's not much I can say politically that won't be partisan, nor will
it be original.

However, I can't help but feel an incredible sense of opportunity.

One year ago (Oct. 2023), the Baha'i community received an uncommonly dense 27-page missive from
the governing body of the Baha'i Faith, a retrospective of sorts of the past hundred years
of Baha'i history. Buried near the end of it was this
bombshell (ok, this is what passes for a bombshell in the Baha'i Faith):

    In _The Advent of Divine Justice_, Shoghi Effendi explained to the Baha'is
    of America that, given the restricted size of their community and the limited
    influence it wileded, they must focus, at that time, on its own growth and
    development as it learned to apply the Teachings. He promised, however, that
    the time would come when they would be called upon to engage their fellow
    citizens in a process of working for the healing and betterment of their
    nation. That time has now come.

We have received some initial signs of this new mode of operations, and many
Baha'is have begun working with like-minded organizations. We must continue
to do that, while taking the opportunity to actually drive discourse. From this point
of view, the opportunity lies in areas where civic society has been dangerously
weakening or backsliding. We can help in two areas:

1. Strengthening existing organizations working for peace, justice and unity;
2. Developing the next generations to educate them in these ideals.

For these generic, uplifting-sounding goals to be translated into action, we
must think concretely. What can we do, and where lie our opportunities?
Here is an informal analysis.

### 1. Common causes:

Networking with like-minded groups brings a number of advantages. It allows us to help
lead discourse in a community, without forcing us to take a visible leadership position.
It helps assist in our cause. Finally, it brings us into contact with those who may be
receptive to our Faith.

Promising avenues for like-minded communities:
- Antimaterialism (world-view), anticompetitiveness
- Spirituality - prayer, meditation (devotions - do, don't say)
- Race unity
    - Related: historical studies
- Antifacism - antimilitarism
- Communitarianism - is it still a thing
- "Internationalism", counterweights to nationalism
- Anti-propaganda
- "Independent investigation of truth"; e.g. accurate reporting, fighting misinformation
- Anti-gambling (probably a new opportunity)
- Political reform. Must be careful to avoid partisan issues.
    - Democratic reform - based on Baha'i governance
    - Anti-corruption (c.f. Secret of Divine Civilization)
- Social justice, generally
- Anti-drug, alcohol
    - Maybe reinvent the historically-significant anti-temperance movement?
- Antimaterialism (philsophy) - monism, theology, dualism
- Philosophy - metaphysic, teleology
- "Practical" Theology
- Participative governance (not simply "consumer democracy")
- Democratic experimentation; e.g. rule by sortition, juries, proportional representation
- Improving civic discourse and debate; consultation
- Groups for nonpartisan politics, against partisan politicking
- Anti-gambling. This will be a new frontier IMO.

### 2. Developmental Ideas

One area where we have been told to lead is through our educational activities - both children's
classes and junior youth. In particular, junior youth are at a very critical age, where the
messages of a materialist society bear down on them with far fewer moderating influences than
in their childhood years. Children's classes, meanwhile, allow us to emphasize the virtues
necessary for a harmonious society and bring us into contact with parents concerned about the
direction of society.

Ideas particularly suited for youth education:
- anti-materialism
- critical thinking
- men's concerns (c.f. "Good Men Project")
- moral development
- responsible leadership
- "mental habits" e.g. phone restraint
- spirituality (like "mindfulness" but much less insipid)
- civic education
