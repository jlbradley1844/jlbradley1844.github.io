## OK, Maybe Not: Correcting My AI Hot Take
A good three years ago, I wrote a blog post, in which I predicted that Generative AI would
result in a productivity null set, as the "killer app" for generative AI at the time seemed
to be poor quality output. Some of this has really turned out to be true. The term "AI slop"
wasn't around when I wrote that - and now it's ubiquitous. Among anybody plugged in,
generative AI output is generally seen as cheap, low-quality, and _declasse_.

Where I was wrong: it appears it as rather useful _in the exact field I work in_.

In my defense, when I wrote that, Claude Code was one year away from release; indeed,
the project itself was something of a lark, developed with little fanfare inside Anthropic
and from which it spread like wildfire. The "killer app" turned out not to be the GPT
engine iself, but its harness, a decidedly non-AI innovation that guided the AI agent
and kept it from flying off the rails. Paired with increasing improvements in engines
and a deep rumoured "secret sauce" database of known good coding practices against
which it could be calibrated, it showed
that generative AI could improve productivity, provided it was used in a context where
its many mistakes could be worked around. So my bad.

It is an open question what is next though. The goal, as far as I can tell from the writings
of the billionaire Thiel-adjacent edgelords, was to bring to heel all these overpaid senior
software developers, with their bothersome beliefs in "rule of law" and "social
responsibility". Certainly, many still think that mass unemployment is a possiblity, as they
loudly warn "oh noes, mass unemployment is on the horizon!!", all the while barely concealing
their under-the-breath sinister caclking. Unfortunately, the reality is the technology has made those
overpaid seniors even _more_ productive and powerful. Far from finishing the job that Indian
oursourcing was meant to do, it instead seems to be getting rid of Indian outsourcing.
So much for having an army of lackeys doing your bidding.

So back to my point - while I was wrong, I maintain that Fred Brooks is still right. Generative
AI still cannot know your business. Your job isn't under threat from an AI. It's under threat
from other senior engineer with an AI. Fortunately, it's pretty easy to figure out how to fight
back when everyone has access to the same AI.
